Passphrase Generator: Diceware-Style Random Words

How it works

Passphrases are random words chosen from the EFF short wordlist of 1,296 words, which works out to about 10.3 bits of entropy per word. Six words give about 62 bits and are much easier to type and remember than a random string.

How to use it

  1. Choose the number of words (5–7 is a good range).
  2. Pick a separator and capitalization style, and optionally add a number.
  3. Generate, then copy or download your passphrase.

Frequently asked questions

Are passphrases as secure as passwords?

Yes, when the words are picked truly at random. A 7-word passphrase from this list has about 72 bits of entropy, comparable to a 12-character random password.

Can I pick my own words instead?

Words people choose themselves are far less random. Let the generator choose and use what it gives you.

What is Diceware?

A method that uses dice to choose words from a numbered list. Mintkit does the same with your device's cryptographic random generator.
